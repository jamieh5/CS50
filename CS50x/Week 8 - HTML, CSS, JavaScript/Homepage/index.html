History

The beginnings (1950s)

Formula 1 as a World Championship began in 1950. The first race took place on 13 May 1950 at Silverstone in Great Britain. Giuseppe Farina won the first Drivers Championship that year with Alfa Romeo. The 1950s belonged to Juan Manuel Fangio from Argentina, who won five titles in total, a record that stood for decades. Cars had front-mounted engines and almost no safety equipment.

Innovation and danger (1960s-1970s)

Rear-engined cars took over in the early 1960s, changing the sport for good. The 1970s brought aerodynamic ideas such as wings and ground effect, but also many fatal accidents. Drivers like Jackie Stewart campaigned for better safety, from track barriers to medical care, and his work changed how the sport thought about risk.

The rivalry era (1980s-1990s)

Turbo engines produced huge power in the 1980s. The rivalry between Ayrton Senna and Alain Prost became one of the sports most famous. On 1 May 1994, Senna died in a crash at Imola, a weekend that also claimed the life of Roland Ratzenberger. It led to major safety reforms that continue to shape the sport.

Dominance and technology (2000s)

Michael Schumacher won five titles in a row with Ferrari from 2000 to 2004 and ended his career with seven in total. Regulations were adjusted again and again to keep racing close, and teams invested heavily in data and simulation.

The hybrid era (2014-2025)

In 2014, F1 introduced turbo-hybrid power units, combining a combustion engine with energy recovery systems. Mercedes dominated, and Lewis Hamilton matched Schumachers seven titles. In 2018, the halo cockpit protection became mandatory, and it has already saved lives. Since 2017, Liberty Media owns the sport, and the Netflix series Drive to Survive brought a new global audience. Max Verstappen and Red Bull took over as the leading force from 2021.

A new chapter (2026)

Big regulation changes arrived in 2026: new power units with a much larger electrical share, active aerodynamics, and lighter, smaller cars. Cadillac joined as an eleventh team.
